Curriculum structure

Read the images first, then begin clinical rotations

The program is sequenced on purpose. Fellows finish the imaging year before rotations begin, so they arrive with the diagnostic vocabulary and imaging literacy that radiology, orthopedic, neurosurgical and pain management teams expect from a colleague.

Year One · Phase I

Advanced Imaging Certification

The complete 18-module Spine Management Pathway, developed with Medality™ and delivered on MRI Online.

  • Spine, neurologic and musculoskeletal MRI, taught by fellowship-trained radiologists
  • Didactic lecture and DICOM case review in every module
  • A research review and 350-word evidence summary per module
  • 5 MRI case submissions per module, building a 90-case portfolio

Year Two · Phase II

Interprofessional Clinical Rotations

Six rotations coordinated within your own community with the help of NSMG, in the settings where spine patients are actually managed.

  • One on-site day per week for six weeks per rotation
  • Live procedures observed, then applied to your own patients that same week
  • 30+ peer-reviewed articles per rotation, with evidence summaries
  • Ends with the fellow working as a spine-care navigator on the team

Year Two · Clinical rotations

How every rotation works

Each rotation runs one day a week for six weeks with a clinical host in your own community, coordinated with the help of NSMG. Fellows keep their own practice going between on-site days, which is the point: what they see on clinical rotations goes straight into how they manage their patients.

1

Observe on site

One day a week with the host service, observing live procedures, consults and clinical decision-making.

2

Read the evidence

Work through the rotation's 30+ peer-reviewed articles and write evidence summaries that tie the literature to what you saw.

3

Apply it that week

Bring the observations into active patient management in your own practice, the same week.

Questions

Fellowship in Spine Management: frequently asked questions

How long is the Fellowship in Spine Management?

Two years, post-doctoral. Year One is the Advanced Imaging Certification (18 imaging modules). Year Two is six interprofessional clinical rotations.

Who is the fellowship for?

Doctors of chiropractic: established community-based practitioners who want advanced imaging credentials, medicolegal standing and working relationships with health systems; recent graduates who want structured post-doctoral training; and chiropractors pursuing teaching roles, expert-witness work, or spine-care leadership in health systems and academic institutions.

How do the clinical rotations work?

There are six rotations. Each runs one day a week for six weeks with a clinical host in your own community, coordinated with the help of NSMG. Fellows observe live procedures, consults and clinical decision-making on site, work through 30+ peer-reviewed articles per rotation with evidence summaries, and apply what they learn to their own patients the same week.

Can I keep my practice running during the fellowship?

Yes. Fellows keep their own practice going between on-site days, which is the point: what they see on clinical rotations goes straight into how they manage their patients.

Where do the rotations take place?

Rotations are coordinated within your own community with the help of NSMG. NSMG works with you to arrange host sites near your practice, so rotation order and host sites depend on the clinical services available in your area.

What credentials do fellows earn?

A Fellowship Completion Certificate from NSMG; the Advanced Imaging Certification, with its continuing-education hours; documented completion of six interprofessional clinical rotations; CV entry documentation for every completed module and rotation; and a qualification pathway toward FSBT Fellowship designation.
